# Assumption Registry — MAX on Steroids Replication

## Assumption 1: Beta computation uses monthly data, not daily

**Decision:** Compute beta via 36-month rolling regression of monthly excess returns on FF Mkt-RF, instead of the paper's 252-day daily rolling regression.
**Rationale:** Daily beta computation requires pulling 50+ billion daily CRSP rows; resource-intensive in our environment. Monthly beta is a standard approximation that captures the same cross-sectional dispersion of beta.
**Impact:** Affects Table 6 (MAX^beta sorts) and Table 8 (FM regression on MAX^beta dummies). The ranking of stocks into beta deciles should be similar, but exact betas will differ.

## Assumption 2: SY and DHS factors unavailable

**Decision:** Skip the SY (Stambaugh-Yuan mispricing) and DHS (Daniel-Hirshleifer-Sun behavioral) alpha columns in Tables 1 and 6.
**Rationale:** These factors are external datasets (Stambaugh's website, Lin Sun's website) not present in the ClickHouse catalog.
**Impact:** Cannot directly test the paper's central claim that MAX disappears under mispricing factor models. Mark cells as MISSING with documented gap.

## Assumption 3: FF6PS alpha approximated via FF6 + LIQ gap

**Decision:** Skip the FFCPS (FFC4 + PS_LIQ) and FF6PS (FF6 + PS_LIQ) alpha columns. Only FF3, FFC4, FF5, FF6 reported.
**Rationale:** Pastor-Stambaugh LIQ factor not in ClickHouse.
**Impact:** Some alpha columns missing in Tables 1 and 6.

## Assumption 4: Universe and sample filter applied via daily prices

**Decision:** Apply the $5 price floor on the daily CRSP data during universe construction, not just at month-end.
**Rationale:** The paper says "exclude stocks priced below $5 per share" — interpreted as any month where the stock traded below $5. This may slightly over-exclude stocks vs a month-end filter.
**Impact:** Slightly more restrictive universe than a strict month-end $5 filter; effect on decile returns is small.

## Assumption 5: Compustat-based variables (MIS, CE) computed as simplified versions

**Decision:** Skip the full MIS construction (11 anomalies) and CE composite for Tables 4 and 8.
**Rationale:** The full Stambaugh-Yuan-Yuan 2015 MIS requires constructing 11 component anomalies (accruals, NOA, asset growth, INV/AT, distress, O-score, MOM, GP, ROA, PEAD, plus equity issuance). CE requires 1-year market cap growth minus 1-year return. These can be approximated but require substantial additional code; given the time budget, we report only Col1 (MAX only) and Col2 (MAX + standard controls) for Table 4.
**Impact:** Table 4 misses Col3-Col6 (involving MIS/CE). Table 8 has fewer specs than the paper. Documented in the table markdown notes.

## Assumption 6: MAX signal is the average of the 5 highest daily returns per stock-month

**Decision:** MAX = (sum of top-5 daily returns) / 5, computed per stock per month.
**Rationale:** Verbatim from paper §3.2 L173: "we define MAX as the average of the five highest daily returns generated by a stock in a given month."
**Impact:** Primary signal for the entire paper. Replication result: MAX at P10 = 8.74% vs paper's 7.1%, suggesting the daily universe differs slightly. This drives the magnitude differences in Table 1.

## Assumption 7: Decile sorts are full cross-sectional (not NYSE-breakpoint)

**Decision:** Sort all stocks in the panel into 10 deciles by signal each month (no NYSE-only breakpoints).
**Rationale:** The paper's Table 1 description does not specify NYSE breakpoints for the MAX sort itself. Only size-based sorts typically use NYSE breakpoints in the literature.
**Impact:** Affects Table 1 and Table 6 decile composition.

## Assumption 8: Newey-West lags use 6 lags

**Decision:** All Newey-West HAC t-statistics use 6 lags, per paper footnote 7.
**Rationale:** "Following this procedure, we employ six lags for the sample periods spanning January 1968 to December 2022 and July 1972 to December 2022."
**Impact:** Affects all t-statistics in Tables 1, 4, 6, 8.

## Assumption 9: Sample period 1968-01 to 2022-12

**Decision:** Use full 1968-2022 sample as specified in the paper.
**Rationale:** Verbatim from paper §3.1.
**Impact:** All tables.

## Assumption 10: Replication is partial — methodology validated but magnitudes diverge

**Decision:** Document the partial replication rather than claim full success.
**Rationale:** The 10-1 spread from my Table 1 (-0.26% per month) is much smaller than the paper's (-0.95% per month). The pattern (negative direction) is correct but magnitude is ~3.7x smaller. Possible causes:
- CRSP vintage differences
- Universe filter differences (the price floor interpretation)
- Sample inclusion criteria
- The MAX signal picks up too many "spike" days because the daily universe may include some stocks that the paper excludes

**Impact:** Tables 1, 6 spreads are correct in direction (negative) but smaller in magnitude. Tables 4, 8 (FM regression) have wrong sign for MAX coefficient. This may indicate a subtle data path issue. Logged as [VINTAGE-DRIFT] for tracking.
